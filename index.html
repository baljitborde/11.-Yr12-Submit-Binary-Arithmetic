🔍 Command words
  • State: give a short answer. No explanation needed.
  • Calculate: work out the answer and show your working.
  • Explain: say what happens and why.
  • Identify: pick out or name the correct item.
  • Show: write out the steps so the method can be seen.

Submit Task: Binary Addition and Subtraction

Pearson Edexcel International AS Computer Science • Unit 1: Principles of Computer Science • Topic 2.2.1 • Year 12 • 30 minutes • 30 marks

AI Strand: CORE (No AI Allowed)

This task checks what you know and can do on your own.

🚫 AI Guidance

AI tools, including Google Gemini, are strictly prohibited for this task.

This task assesses your personal knowledge and understanding. Do not use AI to generate, write, or structure your answers.

  • Do not paste any part of this task into an AI tool, a search engine or a binary calculator.
  • Do not use your pre-learning pages or notes while you answer.
  • If you are stuck, write your best attempt. Working that is partly right still earns marks.

📋 How to complete and submit

  1. Set a timer for 30 minutes.
  2. Work on paper first if it helps you line up the columns.
  3. Type your answers in the Canvas text box, or upload a document or a clear photo of your working.
  4. Label every answer with its question number, e.g. 1, 4(b).
  5. Press Submit Assignment.

Section A: Unsigned binary (13 marks • about 13 minutes)

1. Add these two 8-bit unsigned binary numbers.

   01011101
 + 00110110

Calculate the 8-bit result. Show your carries. [2]

2. A program adds the 8-bit unsigned numbers 11010010 and 01000101.

(a) State the 8-bit result that is stored. [1]

(b) Explain why the stored result is not correct. Use denary values in your answer. [2]

3. Subtract these 8-bit unsigned binary numbers using the column method.

   10100110
 - 00111011

Calculate the 8-bit result. Show where you borrowed. [2]

4. Show how addition can be used to check your answer to Question 3. Write out the addition and its result. [2]

5. A program uses 8-bit unsigned numbers to calculate 01010100 − 01100011 (84 − 99).

Explain why the answer cannot be stored correctly. [2]

6. Identify the part of the CPU that carries out binary addition and subtraction, and name the type of circuit it uses. [2]

Section B: Signed binary (two's complement) (17 marks • about 16 minutes)

All numbers in this section are 8-bit two's complement, with a range of −128 to +127.

7. These two numbers are added: 01011001 (+89) and 11001110 (−50).

(a) State the 8-bit result. [1]

(b) There is a carry out of the most significant bit (MSB). Explain what happens to it, and why the 8-bit result is still correct. [2]

8. A CPU works out 37 − 58. +37 is 00100101.

(a) State −58 in 8-bit two's complement. [1]

(b) Calculate the 8-bit result of adding +37 to your answer to part (a). [1]

(c) Convert your answer to part (b) to denary. Show your working. [1]

9. These two numbers are added: 01001100 (+76) and 01000111 (+71).

(a) State the 8-bit result. [1]

(b) Explain why this result is incorrect. Refer to the signs of the numbers in your answer. [2]

10. A CPU calculates 45 − (−19). +45 is 00101101.

(a) The CPU negates −19 before adding. State the 8-bit binary number it adds to +45. [1]

(b) State the 8-bit result. [1]

11. An 8-bit adder calculates 10110100 + 01011000.

(a) State the 8-bit result that is kept. [1]

(b) Explain whether this result is correct if the numbers are unsigned, and whether it is correct if they are signed. Use denary values in your answer. [3]

12. Explain how using two's complement means the ALU does not need a separate circuit for subtraction. [2]
